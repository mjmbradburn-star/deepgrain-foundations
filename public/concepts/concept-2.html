Chapter I

The licences were never the problem.

Most companies we meet have already spent the money. Seats rolled out to the whole function, a launch email, a training video. Three months later the only thing that changed is the invoice. The work itself - the queue of onboarding tasks, the policy questions, the weekly reports - runs exactly as it did.

The instinct is to buy better tools. The fix is narrower and harder: read how the work actually moves, find the twenty percent of it that repeats, and build for that. A prompt library cannot do it. A champion inside the team, given air cover and a method, can.

Chapter II

Read the grain before you cut.

A carpenter who cuts against the grain splits the wood. Consultancies that build against the grain split the company. So the first thirty days are spent inside the operating cadence: structured interviews, process observation, watching where energy flows and where it stalls. The org chart is rarely how decisions actually get made.

Only then do we build, and never alone. Three or four champions from inside the function work alongside us on their own processes, in their own tools. They learn the craft by shipping, which is the only way it transfers.

MethodRead, then Craft, then Scale. In that order, always. The sequence is the product.
ChampionsSenior coordinators, People Partners, ops leads. People you already pay, given a different mandate. No engineers required.
