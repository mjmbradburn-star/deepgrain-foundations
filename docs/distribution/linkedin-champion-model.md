**Headline:** You don't need engineers to build AI capability inside People.

**Body:**

The most common reason People functions stall on AI: waiting for engineering to free up.

You don't need them. Not for most of what would actually move the needle. You need three or four champions, given air cover and time.

A champion is:

- An operator already inside the function, who knows the work
- Given 20 to 30 percent of their week, formally
- Coached on how to build with AI, not just use it
- Held to a build-first remit: ship one workflow per quarter

What stops it working isn't talent. It's three predictable things:

1. The 20 percent is informal, so it gets eaten by BAU
2. The champion has no one to ask when the build gets hard
3. The first workflow is too ambitious; nothing ships

Fix those and you have a function that compounds. Skip them and you have three exhausted people apologising to their manager.

Engineering is still useful for the harder integrations. But the building of capability is yours, and the people who will do it best are already in your function.
